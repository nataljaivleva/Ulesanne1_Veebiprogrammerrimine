📚 History

Estonia has a rich and complex history spanning thousands of years. The territory was first inhabited after the last Ice Age, around 11,000 years ago.

Ancient and Medieval Period

The ancestors of modern Estonians, the Ests, established themselves in the region during the Bronze Age. The medieval period saw the arrival of Scandinavian and German traders, and eventually the Northern Crusades led to the establishment of the Danish Duchy of Estonia and the German-dominated territories.

Russian and Swedish Rule

Following medieval conflicts, Estonia came under Swedish control in the 17th century, followed by Russian rule after the Great Northern War (1700-1721). The Russian period lasted until 1918.

Independence and Soviet Era

Estonia declared independence in 1918 after World War I. However, the country was occupied by Soviet forces in 1940 during World War II. The Soviet occupation lasted until 1991, when Estonia regained independence through the peaceful "Singing Revolution." Estonia has since developed into a thriving digital society and joined the European Union in 2004.

🌟 Interesting Facts

🖥️ E-Governance Pioneer

Estonia is a global leader in digital innovation. Almost all government services are available online, and citizens can vote via internet since 2005.

🌲 Forest Coverage

Over 50% of Estonia's land is covered by forests, making it one of the most forested countries in Europe. These forests are vital to the nation's ecology and economy.

🎵 Singing Culture

Estonia has a deep tradition of choral singing. The Laulupidu (Song Festival) is held every five years and brings together tens of thousands of singers.

💾 Skype & Tech Startups

Skype was founded in Estonia in 2003. The country has produced numerous tech startups and is known as a hub for innovation and entrepreneurship.

🏛️ UNESCO World Heritage

Tallinn's Old Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, featuring medieval architecture and historic charm with cobblestone streets and centuries-old buildings.

🌊 Islands and Lakes

Estonia has over 2,000 islands and numerous lakes. Lake Peipus is one of the largest freshwater lakes in Europe and serves as a natural border with Russia.
